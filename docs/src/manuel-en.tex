\section{Why Da Capo}

Finale has not been developed since 2024. It still works, but no update will ever repair it if
Windows breaks it one day. Its \fichier{.mus} and \fichier{.musx} files \textbf{only open in
Finale}. The way out is called \textbf{MusicXML}: an open format read by MuseScore Studio (free),
Dorico and Sibelius.

Finale can export a score to MusicXML, but one at a time. With hundreds of scores, that means days of
clicking. \textbf{Da Capo does the clicking for you}, score after score, while you do something
else — and checks the result.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.35}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{>{\bfseries\color{bordeaux}}l X}
\toprule
1. Find & every Finale score in the chosen folders, subfolders included. \\
2. Copy & each one into the working folder, copy checked byte for byte. \\
3. Export & Finale opens each \emph{copy}, exports it to MusicXML (and a reference PDF), closes it. \\
4. Check & every MusicXML is read back: parts, bars, notes, lyrics, dynamics. \\
5. Prepare & every MusicXML becomes a MuseScore score (\fichier{.mscz}) + a PDF to compare. \\
6. Report & \fichier{rapport.html}: what worked, what failed, what deserves a look. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\begin{securitebox}
\begin{itemize}
\item The original files are \textbf{never opened or modified}: Finale only sees copies.
\item The copies are checked (SHA-256 fingerprint) and made read-only.
\item Nothing is sent over the Internet: everything happens on your computer.
\end{itemize}
\end{securitebox}

\section{Before you start}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Install Da Capo}: \fichier{DaCapo-Setup.exe} on Windows (no administrator password).
      On first launch, Windows may show “Windows protected your PC”: \menu{More info} →
      \menu{Run anyway}.
\item \textbf{Install MuseScore Studio} (optional but recommended) from \fichier{musescore.org}
      — not \fichier{musescore.com}, a partly paid sheet-music site. The software is free.
\item \textbf{Open the Finale that Da Capo will use once by hand}, especially if it is never used:
      answer what it asks on first launch (authorisation, update, welcome window), then
      \textbf{close it}.
\item \textbf{Before Finale 25}, the reference PDF goes through the free virtual printer
      \textbf{CutePDF Writer}: check that it is installed.
\end{enumerate}

\astuce{If several Finale versions are installed, Da Capo suggests the most recent: it opens files
from older versions without trouble and writes a much more faithful MusicXML (version 4.0 for Finale
27, versus a “basic MusicXML” for Finale 2003 to 2010).}

\section{The four steps}

\capture[0.78]{../capture_prete-en.png}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=5mm,font=\sffamily\bfseries\color{bordeaux}]
\item[1. Where are your Finale scores?]
\menu{Add a folder…}, or drag the folder from Explorer. All subfolders are searched. Da Capo
immediately counts the scores found (\fichier{.mus} up to Finale 2011, \fichier{.musx} after).
Finale's backup copies (“Backup of…”) are ignored.
\item[2. Where to put the result?]
An empty folder, on a disk with room to spare (allow three to four times the size of the scores).
Everything goes there, in the same tree as your original folders.
\item[3. With which Finale?]
The most recent is suggested and recommended. The first time, click \menu{Diagnostics…}
(section~\ref{sec:diagnostic}).
\item[4. What to produce?]
\textbf{MusicXML}, always. The \textbf{reference PDF}: the score exactly as Finale prints it — your
yardstick for checking, and an archive that will always open. \textbf{MuseScore files}, if MuseScore
Studio is installed.
\end{description}

Then click \menu{Start}.

\section{During the export}

\capture[0.78]{../capture_export-en.png}

Finale opens and works on its own: you can see it open each score, export, close. \textbf{Do not
touch the mouse or keyboard}: a click at the wrong moment would divert a dialog box. Allow from a few
seconds to a minute per score; for several hundred, start in the evening and let it run overnight.
The computer does not go to sleep.

\begin{itemize}
\item \menu{Stop} finishes the current score, then stops cleanly.
\item The button then becomes \menu{Resume}: Da Capo skips everything already done. Even after a
      power cut, it only redoes the score that was in progress.
\item If Finale shows a message (old file to convert, missing font…), Da Capo reads it, records it in
      the report, then closes it. To “Save changes?”, it always answers \textbf{No}.
\item If Finale stops responding, Da Capo stops it, restarts it and moves on to the next score; the
      stubborn score is marked as failed and will be retried on resume.
\end{itemize}

\section{Checking a score}

Open the \textbf{reference PDF} (Finale) and the \textbf{MuseScore PDF} side by side. Notes,
rhythms, lyrics, dynamics and parts come through MusicXML well. What often needs touching up in
MuseScore:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \textbf{layout}: system and page breaks, spacing, staff size;
\item \textbf{custom expressions} and rare symbols, special music fonts;
\item the \textbf{shape of slurs} and the fine position of text;
\item anything that only concerns Finale's audio playback (Human Playback).
\end{itemize}
Start with the scores you still use; the others stay archived in three copies (original, MusicXML,
PDF).

\attention{Keep the original \fichier{.mus} files and the copies in the working folder. One day,
a better converter may redo the export from them.}

\section{Diagnostics}\label{sec:diagnostic}

Each Finale version arranges its commands a little differently. Da Capo finds them by name, in
English and in French. The \menu{Diagnostics…} button checks this on your computer: it opens Finale
with a copy of one score, records its menus and windows, then closes everything, without changing
anything.

\capture[0.78]{../capture_diagnostic-en.png}

If everything is recognised, you are ready. Otherwise, \menu{Send the record} sends it to the
developer, who will adapt Da Capo to your Finale; the record is also saved in \fichier{\_dacapo}.

\section{Updates and privacy}

At startup, Da Capo quietly checks for a new version and offers to install it. With your consent, a
report is sent if it runs into a problem: program and system versions, the error; your file names
and user name are hidden. No score is ever sent.

On macOS and Linux, Finale cannot be driven: Da Capo copies the scores, checks MusicXML files
exported elsewhere and prepares the MuseScore files.
